Why This Matters

Cold homes kill. In the UK, excess cold is linked to over 10,000 excess winter deaths annually—predominantly among older people, children, and those with chronic conditions. The relationship between indoor temperature, health outcomes, and fuel poverty forms a complex but solvable puzzle for social housing providers.

This assessment tool bridges the gap between theoretical compliance (Awaab's Law, HHSRS standards, WHO guidance) and real-world tenant experience. It models how three interconnected factors shape the thermal comfort a household achieves:

What Determines Weekly Heating Budget?

A household's available heating budget depends on income, welfare support, and energy bills:

  • • Income & benefits: Tenants in social housing often rely on Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, or state pensions—capped at modest levels.
  • • Energy cost burden: On low income, energy bills can consume 15–30% of disposable income (compared to 3–5% for higher earners).
  • • Standing charges: Electricity standing charges (£50+/month) and gas (£35+/month) consume 40–60% of available heating budget before any kWh is purchased.

£25/week (~£1,300/year) is a realistic estimate for discretionary heating spend in social housing after standing charges and other essential utilities. Some households have £15/week; fewer have £40+/week. This tool uses £25 as the typical scenario, but you can adjust to model your portfolio's actual budget distribution.

The Three Pillars of Thermal Comfort

  • Building Fabric & Ventilation: Wall insulation, air-tightness, thermal mass, and ventilation rates determine how much and how fast heat escapes—and how moisture accumulates indoors. From solid-wall Victorian terraces (HLP 4.5 W/m²K) to Passivhaus retrofits (0.6), the gap is 7.5×. Ventilation is critical: poor air exchange traps moisture and drives condensation and mould proliferation even in warm homes.
  • Heating Technology: Gas boilers, heat pumps, and smart tariffs convert purchased energy into delivered warmth at vastly different efficiency levels. An Octopus Cosy Heat Pump on a smart tariff delivers 4.9× more warmth per kWh purchased than a high-flow gas boiler (3.81 SPF ÷ 0.78 efficiency).
  • Available Budget: Weekly heating spend—shaped by income, benefit levels, and energy prices—determines how many kWh households can afford. At £25/week and 10p/kWh, that buys only ~36 kWh of delivered heat per day, which may not reach 18°C.

Clinical Evidence & Compliance

Multiple evidence bases inform our thermal thresholds:

  • WHO Living Conditions Standard (21°C): Optimal thermal comfort and cardiovascular health across all demographics.
  • Marmot Review (2011): Below 16°C, blood viscosity spikes, heart attack and stroke risk increases sharply. This is the harm boundary.
  • UK HHSRS (Housing Health & Safety Rating System): Excess Cold is a Category 1 Hazard below 18°C in living areas.
  • Awaab's Law (Social Housing Regulation Act 2023): Landlords must investigate and remediate condensation-driven mould within statutory timeframes. Dew-point condensation triggers reliably below 16°C in uninsulated homes.

How the Tool Works

The assessment tool is built on three computational engines:

1. Thermal Steady-State Model

Given an external temperature, building heat loss (floor area × HLP), internal gains (250W occupancy/appliances), and available heating budget, calculates the achieved indoor temperature. Iterates across user inputs in real-time.

2. Heating System Efficiency Matrix

Compares 17 heating system & tariff combinations (gas boilers, heat pumps, electric direct, Octopus smart tariffs). Each has empirically calibrated efficiency curves or COP values. Octopus Cosy Heat Pump SPF 3.81 is drawn from real-world performance data.

3. Degree-Hour Seasonal Model

Projects 30-week heating season (October–April) across six UK regions with region-specific weekly external temperatures. Calculates annual delivered heat demand, cost, and weeks in budget deficit. Flags statutory compliance risks (HHSRS, Awaab's Law).

Real-World Scenario: A Typical Social Housing Property

Consider a 70 m² property in the Midlands (typical UK average climate) with:

  • • Building: Decent Homes standard (HLP 2.0 W/m²K, EPC C)
  • • Heating: Gas boiler on standard tariff (78% efficiency)
  • • Tenant Budget: £25/week available for heating
  • • External Temp: 4°C (typical UK winter day)

Outcome: At £25/week (£22.92 after the gas standing charge), the tenant achieves only ~15.3°C—below the 18°C HHSRS threshold and the 16°C condensation boundary. Reaching 21°C comfort would require ~£38.60/week (a £13.60 deficit). Heating to 18°C in the coldest mid-winter week (January) costs ~£34/week in fuel, an ~£11 shortfall against the net budget.

Policy Lever: Switching to an Octopus Cosy Heat Pump on the Tenant Power tariff reduces the ~£38.60 cost to ~£21.10/week for 21°C comfort—a 45% saving—which the £25 budget can cover. The combination of higher efficiency (3.81 SPF) and tariff discount (30% off electricity) closes the affordability gap.

Key Insights for Housing Providers

  • Budget deficits are endemic, not anomalous. Most households on £15–30/week budgets fall short of 21°C comfort in winter, even with efficient heating. Retrofitting insulation and upgrading heating technology are not optional nice-to-haves—they are statutory compliance pathways.
  • Heat pump + smart tariff + smart controls = the affordability multiplier. The combination of higher efficiency, off-peak pricing, and automated comfort delivers 50–60% savings vs. gas boilers on standard tariffs. Octopus Tenant Power amplifies this further.
  • Standing charges are often invisible debt. A 30p/day electricity standing charge (£21/month) can consume 80%+ of a low heating budget before a single kWh is purchased. Deducting standing charges from heating budgets shows the true financial cliff.
  • Awaab's Law compliance is thermodynamic, not procedural. Homes below 16°C will develop condensation and mould regardless of tenant ventilation habits. Investment in fabric insulation and heating capacity is the only pathway to statutory safety.

⚠️ Tool Caveat & Limitations

This assessment tool is designed for indicative modelling and policy discussion in the social housing context. It provides physics-based estimates of thermal comfort, heating costs, and health compliance based on simplified building models and average climate data.

The tool is not: A professional engineering assessment, a guarantee of actual heating performance, a substitute for Building Regulations compliance analysis, or tailored advice for specific properties or residents. Real-world heating outcomes depend on occupant behaviour, ventilation practices, heating system commissioning, building defects, and weather variation not fully captured here.

For compliance and retrofit decisions: consult a building surveyor, energy assessor, or qualified heating engineer. This tool is offered to help housing providers understand policy trade-offs and identify priority intervention areas.
